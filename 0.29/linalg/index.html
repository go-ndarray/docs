linalg — numpy.linalg in pure Go

Solve, Inv, Det, Cholesky, QR, SVD, Eig and the rest of numpy.linalg: the algorithms, their accuracy against numpy 2.5.3, and their speed.

You are reading the documentation of an outdated version, whose text may no longer be accurate. Go to the latest version.

Mirrored from docs/linalg.md at v0.29.0.

Package linalg gives ndarray arrays the functions of numpy.linalg, written in Go without cgo or assembly of its own: the matrix products it relies on are the ndarray package’s packed SIMD GEMM.

Semantics

  • Stacks. The last two axes are the matrix; leading axes are a stack, processed one matrix per goroutine. Solve broadcasts the stack dimensions of a and b as numpy’s gufunc does; a 1-D b is one vector, any other b a stack of matrices (numpy 2’s rule).
  • Dtypes. As in numpy, everything is computed in double precision: float64 and complex128 directly, integers and bools promoted to float64, float32 and complex64 widened. Results have numpy’s dtypes: single precision in, single precision out (rounded from the double result, which is what numpy does too — its linalg gufuncs also run in double); singular values, Hermitian eigenvalues, logabsdet and norms are real; Eig is always complex (numpy 2). MatrixPower with n ≥ 0 keeps an integer dtype.
  • Conventions. LAPACK’s: Householder reflectors with β = −sign(α)‖x‖, so R’s diagonal can be negative as in numpy; ascending Hermitian eigenvalues; decreasing singular values; Eig vectors with unit norm and their largest component real, conjugate vectors for conjugate eigenvalues of a real matrix (dgeev’s normalization).
  • Not unique. Signs and phases of eigenvectors and singular vectors, and any orthonormal basis of a repeated eigenvalue’s space, may differ from numpy’s. Compare A·v = λ·v, A = U·S·Vh, not the vectors.

Algorithms, and why

All are LAPACK’s, re-expressed for row-major storage. Each is written once, generic over float64 | complex128; Go 1.27 stencils the two and folds the type switches of the helpers (conj, abs, …), so the real code pays nothing for the complex case (measured: same speed as a hand-specialised loop).

The SVD: why Golub–Kahan with QR, and divide and conquer. The choice was between Golub–Kahan bidiagonalization followed by the implicit-shift QR iteration, and one-sided Jacobi. One-sided Jacobi is the more accurate in a strong sense — it computes the small singular values of a matrix whose columns are badly scaled to high relative accuracy (Demmel–Veselić) — but it needs 5–10 sweeps of O(n³) rotations, several times the work, and it is not what numpy computes. Golub–Kahan reduction is backward stable: the computed singular values are those of A + E with ‖E‖ ≈ ε‖A‖, so each is within about ε·σ₁ of the truth — numpy’s (gesdd’s) accuracy, exactly. The QR iteration (bdsqr, with Demmel and Kahan’s zero shift) then finds the singular values of the bidiagonal to high relative accuracy, so it adds nothing to that error. It is used at the leaves of the divide and conquer and whenever the vectors are not wanted. With vectors, the QR iteration’s plane rotations cost O(n³) of non-vectorized work (5.8 s at n = 1024 in the first version); divide and conquer, LAPACK’s gesdd algorithm and the one numpy calls, puts that work into GEMMs (0.26 s now). Its singular vectors stay orthogonal because the updating vector is recomputed from the computed roots (Gu and Eisenstat), and the secular equation is solved with each distance dᵢ − σ formed from the nearest pole, in the squared form (dᵢ − σ)(dᵢ + σ).

Hermitian eigenproblems follow the same pattern: the tridiagonal QL/QR iteration (steqr) is used without vectors and for orders up to 25, divide and conquer (stedc) above, which took n = 1024 from 3.6 s to 0.13 s.

General eigenproblems use the classical double-shift QR (dlahqr), not LAPACK’s multishift QR with aggressive early deflation (dlaqr0–5). That is the largest gap to numpy, at large orders (below).

Accuracy and verification

Against numpy. linalg/testdata/cases.py generates 1,040 cases with numpy 2.5.3 (uv run --with numpy python linalg/testdata/cases.py > linalg/testdata/cases.json; the JSON is embedded in the test binary, so the emulated CI architectures read it from anywhere). They cover every function; float64, float32, complex128, complex64, int32/int64 and bool inputs; 0×0, 1×1, tall, wide, stacked and broadcast shapes; Hilbert matrices (κ up to 10¹⁶), graded and rank-deficient matrices, exactly singular ones, non-symmetric matrices with complex eigenvalues, Jordan blocks and other defective matrices, NaN and Inf inputs; and every error numpy raises on them (124 cases). The test checks, for each case:

  • errors: an error of the same kind (ErrSingular, ErrNotPosDef, ErrNoConvergence, ErrLinAlg for non-finite input to Eig, ErrShape, ErrArgs);
  • dtype and shape of every result, exactly;
  • unique values (solutions, inverses, determinants, Cholesky factors, singular values, eigenvalues, least-squares solutions and residuals, pseudo-inverses, ranks, norms, condition numbers, powers, products) elementwise, within 32·n·ε·κ of the largest entry of the result, with κ the condition number of the problem (1 for singular values, Hermitian eigenvalues and norms; cond(A) for solves and inverses; the effective condition over the kept singular values for Lstsq and Pinv, squared for the residuals), plus one float32 rounding for single-precision results;
  • factors that are not unique through their identities: ‖QᴴQ − I‖, ‖A − QR‖/‖A‖, R upper triangular; ‖UᴴU − I‖, ‖VhVhᴴ − I‖, ‖A − U·S·Vh‖/‖A‖; ‖VᴴV − I‖, ‖AV − VΛ‖/‖A‖ with ascending eigenvalues; for Eig, ‖Av − λv‖/‖A‖ per vector, unit norm, largest component real, conjugate pairs exactly conjugate — all within 32·n·ε (ε of the result’s dtype);
  • general eigenvalues matched to numpy’s nearest unused one, within 100 times how far numpy’s own eigenvalues move when each entry is perturbed by 10⁻¹⁴ of itself (an empirical condition number, recorded in the fixture), so a Jordan block’s eigenvalue, accurate only to ε^(1/4), is judged by what is achievable.

914 cases are value-checked and 124 are errors; the 2 deliberate departures below are logged.

Beyond the fixture (properties_test.go, kernels_test.go): the same identities at orders 100 and 131 (above the blocking and divide-and-conquer thresholds), real and complex, tall and wide; a stack of 16 matrices run in parallel equals the matrices run one by one; repeated eigenvalues and singular values, rank-deficient and zero matrices (the deflation paths); cyclic permutations, Grcar and Frank matrices, orthogonal matrices, high-order Jordan blocks and coupled rotation blocks (exceptional shifts, overflow rescaling in the back-substitution); balancing at the limits of the exponent range; every iteration forced to fail (ErrNoConvergence from each function); and the 2×2 kernels (laev2, las2, lasv2, lanv2, lartg, solve2) checked through their defining identities on the inputs that reach their special cases.

Negative controls. Dropping the row interchange in the LU leaf (no partial pivoting) fails 64 fixture cases — 23 solve, 17 inv, 10 det, 10 slogdet, 4 matrix_power — and the order-100 identity test. Loosening the secular-equation solver’s stopping test by 10⁸ fails the four order-34 eigh cases (eigenvalues off by ~10⁻¹¹ relative) and the zero-diagonal bidiagonal divide-and-conquer test (‖B − USVᵀ‖ of 10⁻⁸).

Coverage is 100% of statements under the CI gate (go test -race -coverpkg=./... ./...).

numpy is faster on single matrices, by 1.5–4× (7× for eig at 1024). Most of that is the matrix product underneath: on the same host the ndarray GEMM takes 18 ms for a 1024² product where Accelerate (which uses the matrix coprocessor) takes 3.8 ms, 0.37 ms against 0.10 ms at 256. The blocked algorithms here spend most of their time in that GEMM, so their gap to numpy is smaller than the GEMM’s. The exceptions:

  • eig at large n: the classical double-shift QR is O(10n³) of scalar work; LAPACK’s multishift QR with aggressive early deflation moves it into GEMMs. This is the main follow-up.
  • small orders (64): fixed costs (conversion of the input, allocation, the packing of a small GEMM) weigh more than the arithmetic.

Stacks of small matrices run in parallel — numpy’s gufuncs loop over them on one core — and there this package is faster (median of 3, same host):

What is left

  • Multishift QR with aggressive early deflation for Eig (the 7× gap at n = 1024), and a blocked Hessenberg reduction.
  • A faster small-matrix path (n ≤ 64): unblocked kernels without the GEMM packing, fewer copies.
  • The hermitian options and rtol arguments above, and the tensor functions.
  • Eigh on NaN input returning NaNs like numpy, if that is preferred.
